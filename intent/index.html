the intent plane — fail-closed authorization

Agents propose.
The gate disposes.
The record re‑verifies — without trusting the gate.

Before an AI agent moves money, files a report, or triggers a workflow, it must declare the intent. A deterministic gate authorizes it against a policy specification a human signed — or refuses. Every decision lands as exactly one durable record, built to be independently recomputed later. The worst case is an action that wrongly waits, never one that wrongly executes.

without

Authorization lives inside the agent's runtime — per-adapter checks, config nobody signed. Audit reads the logs after the fact, and has to trust the code that wrote them.

with the plane

Authorization sits outside the agent: criteria arrive only through a signed, content-addressed spec; scoring is tri-state and fail-closed; the record re-derives from the feed alone — by someone who trusts nothing.

What it refuses: anything unevaluable. Missing data, an unsigned or revoked spec, an empty criteria set, a duplicate action — all deny.

the supply side, in one rule

Declare once at the framework layer — every agent inherits it. The declarant derives idempotency keys deterministically from action identity (never a fresh UUID per attempt), classifies every terminal totally over the closed cause vocabulary — anything outside it fails closed as Unknown — and on an HTTP 500 consults the per-intent feed before deciding anything. force_scores, the guarded test affordance, exists in no declarant type.

three commitments

One signed object. Authority is key possession. Fail-closed, twice.

Not aspirations — load-bearing claims, each with a named mechanism and a test that refutes it. The claim-by-claim standing lives in docs/assurance.md; nothing asks to be believed.

01One signed object

The bytes the attester signs are the bytes the gate executes — a hash equality, not an alignment of documents. Criteria cannot ride the wire at all (the field does not exist; the old shape gets a loud 400) — they reach the gate only through signature verification plus content-address equality.

02Authority is key possession

The AI-facing drafting side — the author — holds no keys and structurally cannot sign. Nothing is enforceable until a human attester signs it, and a signed tombstone revokes it: maker-checker, applied to what agents act on. No signing seat exists anywhere in this repo.

03Fail-closed, twice, with one record

Verdicts are pass / fail / unevaluable — which never passes. Volatile facts and the authority itself are re-verified at the last moment before the consequence fires, and the decision and its audit record are one byte-exact event.

⤷ a signed tombstone revokes — a tombstone signed by any other key does not. Revocation is checked at declaration and re-checked at the dispatch edge: a spec pulled mid-flight stops at the edge, and the withdrawal is itself part of the record.

the decision flow

Four moves, four terminals — and every branch fails closed.

Every declared intent walks DECLARED → RESOLVING → ACTIVE → VERIFYING toward exactly one terminal. No FAILED or FAILED_AT_DISPATCH intent ever leaves an ACHIEVED record in the feed — a refused or duplicate intent means no value moved.

01 · declare→

Key required, spec by hash

The declarant supplies the idempotency key, scope, and the spec's content address — criteria cannot ride the wire. An absent key, or an unattested, revoked, or thin spec, refuses at the door: the scorer is never consulted.

02 · verify→

Score tri-state

The gate consults the scorer: every declared criterion lands Pass, Fail, or Unevaluable. Any Fail or Unevaluable denies authorization — unevaluable never collapses into a pass.

03 · re-check→

The dispatch edge

Volatile facts and the authority itself are re-verified immediately before the consequence fires, and the declared key is reserved. Drift, a tombstone, or a key collision ⟹ FAILED_AT_DISPATCH.

04 · record■

One durable answer

One synchronous terminal, one durable record — grant, shadow, or refusal — fsynced before success and carrying its trajectory hash. Consumers settle only from observed ACHIEVED.

the state machine — terminal states in bold, none of them unsafe
statekindmeaning
DECLAREDworkingdeclaration received; the idempotency key and the spec's content address bind. Door refusals — absent key, unattested / revoked / thin spec — answer FAILED synchronously
RESOLVING → ACTIVEworkingthe signed spec resolves — signature verified, bytes hashing to the claimed address — and the intent is admitted for scoring
VERIFYINGworkingtri-state scoring over every declared criterion, via the plane's scorer
ACHIEVEDterminalthe one durable record, fsynced before success — consumers settle from it
SHADOW_RECORDEDterminalfully scored, durably recorded, not authorized — staged rollout, itself signed; promotion to enforce is a fresh attestation, never a config toggle
FAILEDterminalanything unevaluable, at any step — missing key, unattested or revoked or thin spec, unreachable scorer, failed criterion. No record settles
FAILED_AT_DISPATCHterminala drifted fact, a pulled spec, or a duplicate key at the last moment before the consequence fires. The key is not reserved; nothing settles

Scope — emit-and-observe. The gate's job ends at the durable record. It settles nothing and never calls out; a consumer pulls the feed by cursor and recomputes. A crash on either side loses nothing. And the terminal-position record of every completed authorization — grant, shadow, or refusal — carries its trajectory hash, so a trimmed or edited log is detectable by recomputation.

invariants

Eight properties, enforced by construction and pinned by tests.

None of these is a convention the code tries to follow. Each is structural — violating it fails an acceptance test, not a guideline. Amendments run CONTRACT.md first, then the pinned tables — never the reverse.

01Sole emitter

The gate alone emits the single ACHIEVED record, fsynced to the durable feed before success is reported. Consumers act only after observing it.

02Tri-state, fail-closed

Pass / Fail / Unevaluable — any Fail or Unevaluable ⟹ not authorized. Absence of valid proof is a denial, never a default-pass.

03Stable vs volatile

Stable criteria score once, at declaration. Volatile facts score again at the dispatch edge — same authority, immediately before authorizing.

04Idempotency by construction

The key is required and reserved at the edge. Same key, different intent hash — a near-duplicate — collides and is refused. At-most-once holds structurally.

05Determinism & replay

Per-intent logical clock, IDs from the episode seed, no wallclock. Replay drives a recompute, byte-identical — and the global cursor never enters the trajectory hash.

06Durability

The feed and the key reservations survive restart over the same INTENT_DATA_DIR — kill/restart proven: byte-identical events, same-key re-dispatch still refused.

07Thin-spec defense

Zero criteria or unknown volatility refuse before any scoring. "No criterion failed" is never satisfied by "no criterion existed" — attestation does not launder vacuity.

08Core neutrality

core/ carries no domain vocabulary, pinned mechanically. The kernel knows no domain — the treasury application below is one instantiation, not the design.

the record, re-verified

Hand the feed to someone who doesn't trust you.
They can re-derive all of it.

The plane's cost structure is a revealed preference: logical clocks with no wallclock, a JSON-free length-prefixed hash encoding, byte-frozen cross-language fixtures, an fsync before every success. None of it buys anything at decision time — it pays off when someone who does not trust the gate recomputes the record afterward.

the demand side's seat · verifier/cmd/intent-verifyexit codes are the interface
$ go run ./verifier/cmd/intent-verify core/contract/feed/events-good.jsonl
→ RESULT: VERIFIED · exit 0  # hashes recompute · lifecycle replays · at-most-once holds
$ go run ./verifier/cmd/intent-verify core/contract/feed/events-tampered.jsonl
→ RESULT: REFUTED · exit 1   # one flipped byte — the standing mutant, refuted forever

Two implementations, one report. A Go package and a stdlib-only Python twin produce canonical reports that are byte-compared, and an import pin proves the verifier tree runs none of the gate's code. Verdicts are tri-state like the gate itself — verified · refuted · unverifiable — and unverifiable never passes.

Status, honestly 5 stated›
  • Key authority is test-grade until ADR-0009 — and every signature says so (key_authority: "test").
  • Record signing (R1) and workload identity (R2) are staged, not built — so today the verifier proves the record self-consistent, not never-rewritten.
  • force_scores is guarded and witnessed: refused unless the server booted with INTENT_UNSAFE_FORCE_SCORES=1, and every scored record carries a scorer_id witness — a forced decision is never byte-indistinguishable from a live one. Wherever the flag is set the bypass is total; it must never reach production.
  • The feed read surface is unauthenticated by design — network isolation is a deployment decision the platform owns.
  • The declarant ships in Go; a Python declarant twin is recorded future work.
the reference application · one step from concept to consequence

The same gate, pointed at a real consequence: treasury payments.

The kernel reads no artifacts and knows no domain — criteria, thresholds, and what counts as a duplicate arrive only through the signed spec. Instantiated on treasury payments (action class 1) in the testing monorepo, the loop closes over three hashes: every settlement carries rule_artifact_hash · intent_spec_hash · trajectory_hash — joining the runtime trace back to the exact signed policy that authorized it.

A duplicate of an irreversible payment isn't caught.
It's impossible — refused at the dispatch edge, by construction.

The worked example the monorepo runs end to end: the idempotency key is a declared, first-class gate criterion — required, and reserved at the dispatch edge. The same declared key then keys the settlement ledger, so at-most-once holds end to end.

without

At-most-once is an assertion: dedup lives in each adapter's retry logic, audited after the fact. A retried near-duplicate can execute twice before anyone reads the log.

with the gate

Same key, one changed field ⟹ FAILED_AT_DISPATCH. At-most-once holds by construction, whatever the action is.

policy plane · atlas→

IntentSpec — the signed policy

What authorizes a payment — including what counts as a duplicate — is a signed, expert-attested, registry-governed artifact, published by content hash. Not adapter-local config.

this plane · the gate→

Authorization

The resolver re-verifies the pinned artifact before any criterion is scored — absent, revoked, or hash-mismatched ⟹ fail closed. Then tri-state scoring, the dispatch edge, one durable record.

settlement plane · compass⟲

Settlement — the loop closes

Pulls GET /v2/events?since=cursor and recomputes from observed ACHIEVED records only. Each settlement carries the full trace record — pointing back to the signed policy that started the loop.
